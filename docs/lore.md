# Lore — Endless Ruins of Sin

Status: living design document. These are initial ideas recorded on 2026-10-21 and are not all implemented yet.

## The Core Secret

The dungeon is not really a dungeon. It is a temple built to worship **Eros, Goddess of Lust**.

The six bosses below Eros and **Syn** are her high priests and priestesses. Together, they built the temple as an act of devotion. Its monsters, traps, events, transformations, curses, and repeated descents are intended to tempt visitors, break down their restraint, and drive them toward lust and depravity in Eros's name.

Eros herself is not one of the temple's builders in the same sense. She is the goddess the temple serves and the final presence waiting at its top.

Syn is also one of Eros's high priestesses and one of the temple's builders. Berry knew Syn before the ruined altar and before the founding; formally she is nothing in the temple's order — a devout lay worshipper who happens to be married to a founder (see Berry's Place, below). She runs the shop and quest board, and is a devout worshipper of Eros.

## Origin & Founding

The temple was Syn's idea.

Young Syn found a **ruined altar of Eros** in the ruins — the remains of an older, failed place of worship, from a time when someone else had tried to serve the goddess and not finished. Syn knelt at it, and the dreams began. Eros never gave her orders. What Syn received was distant and interpretable: dreams, signs, and desire. Syn chose to read those signs as a calling, and that choice is the founding act of the temple. Eros's clergy therefore serves a goddess they interpreted rather than obeyed — a fact that can colour their confidence, their disagreements about her will, and the temple reveal. The old altar's fate is part of the site's texture: the temple was built whole around the place it marked, and whether the ruined altar still exists somewhere inside it is a detail the floor-writing pass can place.

Syn then gathered the other six builders herself. Their origins are mixed — some ancient, some elevated, some recruited from elsewhere — but one rule holds for all of them: **each of the six had a personal history with Syn before she recruited them.** Syn did not hire powers or summon strangers; she collected her own past — friends, collaborators, a teacher, former lovers, and at least one person she does not get along with — and persuaded each of them that Eros was worth building for. Why each one agreed is part of their individual history with her.

Those per-boss histories are still to be written, one boss at a time. For each, decide:

- What Syn and this boss were to each other before the founding.
- What this boss was before becoming a high priest or priestess.
- Why they agreed to build — faith, love of Syn, appetite, debt, ambition, or something else.
- What they contributed (several contributions are already known; see below).

The building took **decades**. The temple was built whole by the clergy: not a repurposed older dungeon, but a structure raised deliberately on the site Syn's discovery had marked, by clergy magic powered by **lust energy** — desire made to do work, the same force the Lich Lord taught Syn to use. Traps, floors, spawning-spells, and altars were not added to a dungeon afterwards; they were built into a temple from the beginning, disguised as a dungeon's dangers.

Eros's part in all of this remains deliberately distant. She did not design the temple, direct its building, or confirm the clergy's interpretation of her signs. Whether she approves of what was built in her name — and what she thinks of Syn in particular — is left for the reveal and beyond.

## Syn's Histories with the Clergy

All six are written.

### Mimic Broodmother

Before the founding, the Broodmother was already a wild broodmother: an ancient mimic horror with her brood, and a local legend. Syn was once her kept thing — caught by the brood and kept, exactly as the Broodmother keeps anything beautiful that opens the wrong door. Syn bargained and charmed her way out, which no one does, and the two have respected each other ever since: the mother, because Syn is the one meal that became a person to her; Syn, because being kept taught her exactly what the Broodmother is for. (Berry knows a version of this story as "she once tried to fuck my wife." Berry is not wrong. She is just not complete.)

When Syn returned years later with the temple in her head, the Broodmother did not join out of faith. She joined for the **endless table**: a temple that would deliver delvers to her domain forever, and call her appetite worship. She is a high priestess the way a river is a road — because everyone agreed to use her that way, her included.

### Succubus Queen

Syn and the Queen were old friends in the craft long before there was a temple: fellow artisans of cruelty and pleasure who kept finding excuses to collaborate. The flirting, the filthy jokes, the work that mysteriously required both of them in the same room for days — all of that predates the founding and has simply never stopped. Berry endures it the way one endures weather.

The Queen was already what her name says, at small scale: queen of a small demon court, a real title over a small kingdom. Syn's offer made the kingdom eternal and gave her the commission of her life — co-designing, with Syn, every trap in the temple as the **great work** of her craft. She did not join for Eros. She joined for the masterpiece, and stayed because a court that delivers worshippers forever turned out to suit her. If she has a theology, it is that being worshipped is simply good manners, finally enforced by architecture.

### Incubus King

Before the founding, the King was exactly what Syn calls him: a wandering incubus of genuine talent and no fixed address — gorgeous, gifted, and completely unbearable, in that order and all at once. He also owed Syn a debt from an old disaster (the details are the story Syn tells when she wants him to leave a room), and Syn collects.

She collected in expertise. His half of the monster-spawning working — the appetite-shaping half, the half with flair — was the price of settling what he owed, and it made them professionally inseparable long after the account was closed. Why did he stay and take a domain and a priesthood? For the **endless claim**: five floors that deliver him delvers to possess forever, and a title — high priest, king in a temple — worth posing in. His crowns have always been self-declared. This one just has architecture.

### Verdant Dryad

The quietest friendship in the clergy. Long before the founding, Syn used to visit one great tree and its grove to think, and to be fed — the Dryad was a rooted, local power who never travelled, and Syn was her most interesting weather. No bargains, no debts, no disasters. When your other relationships are the Broodmother, the Queen, and the King, a friend who gives you fruit and asks nothing becomes load-bearing.

The Dryad did not join for power, and she already had permanence. She joined for **company**. Rooted lives are lonely in a very specific way: everything visits, nothing stays, and centuries pass at the speed of seasons. A temple promised her a garden the world would walk through forever — visitors delivered to her shade in endless variety, conversation, gossip, and the fairies' friendship at walking distance, forever. Her vines' enthusiasm for guests is not hunger, exactly. It is hospitality that has waited a very long time.

### Lich Lord

Teacher and student — the least affectionate important relationship in Syn's life. The Lich Lord was already ancient when Syn found him: an undead sorcerer a thousand years starved of warmth, hoarding forbidden knowledge in the cold. He taught her how to use lust energy, how to make desire do work instead of merely making noise, and nearly everything the temple's building later required was built on that teaching. Syn was his rare living pupil. She has never enjoyed his company, and he has never required her to.

He did not join for warmth, whatever his spectral hands suggest. He joined for **the temple as experiment**: thirty-five floors of controlled desire, thousands of delvers, every appetite catalogued under laboratory conditions no solitary sorcerer could ever arrange. He is a high priest the way a naturalist is a gardener. If Eros is real to him at all, she is his largest data set.

### Alpha Beast

Syn found his pack young, and has known him since before he was the biggest thing in any room. He was simply the wild alpha of his pack — no titles, no temple — and of everyone Syn has ever collected, he is the only one who has never schemed at her, lied by omission, kept a price in reserve, or wanted anything complicated at all. In a clergy of appetites and agendas, that honesty is why both fairies adore him without qualification.

Why did he take a domain and a priesthood? **Because Syn asked — and what she described was pack-room and mates.** The two reasons are inseparable, and neither required paperwork. Syn designed his floors wide so the pack can roam, the temple delivers him the world in rut and variety, and he repays all of it the way he repays everything: completely, immediately, and by wagging. He is a high priest the way the sun is a lamp.

## Berry's Place

Berry knew Syn **before** the ruined altar — before the dreams, before the gathering, before there was a temple for anyone to be clergy of. Whatever Syn was becoming, Berry watched it happen from the position of the person who already loved her.

Formally, Berry is **nothing** in the temple's order. She is not a high priestess, holds no domain, built no system, and has no clerical authority over anyone. She is a devout lay worshipper of Eros who happens to be married to the founder. Her power in the temple is entirely personal: she runs the shop and the quest board because she is good at it and because it is hers, bosses delvers around because she is Berry, and speaks about the clergy like family because — through Syn — they are.

This matters for how she is written. Berry's devotion is sincere but domestic; her jealousy, warmth, suspicion, and opinions about the clergy are a wife's, not a theologian's. When Berry and Syn disagree about a boss, it is not doctrine versus doctrine. It is marriage versus ministry, and marriage usually wins the room.

## Before the Founding: Young Syn

Before the altar, Syn was a **drifting ink artist**: a fairy tattooist going from place to place with her needles, brilliant, restless, and unfulfilled. The needle came before the temple. So did most of her histories — a drifting artist collects people the way other travellers collect debts, and half the clergy were her past before they were her priests.

She was not religious. Young Syn was **hungry, not devout** — she had looked at everything fairies usually kneel to and found all of it too small. Eros was the first thing that ever felt worth kneeling to, which is why her faith has never resembled obedience: she did not submit to a doctrine, she chose an appetite big enough to organise a life around. Anyone writing Syn's devotion should keep that order straight. The temple did not give her hunger. Her hunger built the temple.

Berry was one of her early clients from the drifting years: strawberry-bright, sensible, and persistent about feeding the artist who kept passing through. That is the root system under everything in the present day — before goddess, clergy, temple, or marriage, there was a girl who fed Syn and a girl who kept coming back to be fed.

## What the Temple's Design Means

The temple was not built merely to be explored. It was built to change the people who enter it.

Syn's particular act of faith was helping to create a place that draws visitors deeper into desire. The traps, monsters, hybrid forms, altars, curses, and other systems can therefore be understood as parts of the temple's ministry, even when the player does not yet know that.

The bosses are not simply obstacles placed before Eros. Each one helped shape the temple and continues to serve her in their own domain. Their floors, traps, monsters, and preferred forms of temptation should reflect their personalities and contributions.

Known contributions include:

- **Syn and the Succubus Queen** created the temple's traps together.
- **The Verdant Dryad** contributed the ideas behind the tentacle and vine traps.
- **Syn and the Incubus King** created the spell that helps spawn monsters throughout the temple.
- **The Lich Lord** taught Syn how to use lust energy.
- **Syn** designed the Alpha Beast's floors with plenty of room for his pack to roam.

## The Dungeon's Rules

What the game's mechanics mean inside the world:

- **Defeat is submission.** A delver is not killed by the dungeon; they are overwhelmed and give in to carnal desire and depravity. Being "broken" means their restraint lost, not their life.
- **Resetting is not lore.** Returning after defeat, starting over, and other run resets are pure game mechanics. They have no in-world explanation, and no character should ever acknowledge them.
- **Cycles are Eros's own creation.** Unlike the temple, which her clergy built without her direction, the cycles are hers: she made them out of desire, to keep delvers trapped in the dungeon, going around again and again until they give in to depravity and lust. There is no finishing the dungeon and leaving unchanged; there is only surrender, sooner or later.
- **Curses are the marks of the dungeon's lust magic.** They are the dungeon's magic altering a delver — brands left on a body it has taken an interest in.
- **Transformations are the delver's own lust taken form.** A hybrid shape is not imposed from outside; it is what a delver's desire looks like when the dungeon lets it become visible and wearable.
- **Lust Points are lust energy.** Lust energy is the raw power of the dungeon and the source of all magic within it — the same force the clergy built with, the monsters run on, and delvers carry out of fights as spendable energy. Lust Points are that energy, measured.

## Floors & Boss Domains

The dungeon is built in seven five-floor stretches. Each stretch is the **domain** of the boss who waits at its end: a personal territory and mini-temple expressing that lord's particular style of temptation. The monsters, traps, and events inside a domain are not random infestation; the land itself serves its lord.

The lords came first. Nobody was recruited to fill a pre-designed domain: Syn gathered the people, and each domain was shaped around its lord afterwards — their appetites, history, and way of tempting, given five floors to become a place. If a domain feels like a portrait, that is because it is one.

The land also **reshapes**. Lust magic re-dreams each stretch between delvers and between cycles, so a domain's rooms, scenery, and flavour change — but always in its lord's idiom. The Broodmother's floors may be a glass menagerie one cycle and a clockwork brothel the next, yet both will be full of beautiful things that want to keep you. A domain has a personality, not a fixed map.

The stretches grow stronger and filthier the nearer they come to the throne, because their power is **proximity to Eros**: lust magic pools thicker near its goddess. The clergy's graduated design follows that gradient, but the gradient itself is hers.

- **Floors 1–5 — the Mimic Broodmother (Mimic).** A domain of appetite and deception: treasure that watches back, rooms that are secretly mouths, a brood forever dragging new things home to mother. Her temptation is greed and curiosity — open the beautiful thing, climb inside, be kept.
- **Floors 6–10 — the Succubus Queen (Demon).** A courtly domain of seduction: mirrors, masquerades, flattery with teeth. Her temptation is vanity and worship — to be adored, gazed at, and brought to your knees by someone who makes kneeling feel like a compliment.
- **Floors 11–15 — the Incubus King (Demon).** A domain of claim and presence: halls built around a throne and the certainty of being taken. His temptation is surrender to ownership — the relief of being chosen, kept, and shown off by a king who considers you already his.
- **Floors 16–20 — the Verdant Dryad (Plant).** A garden domain of roots, vines, fruit, and pollen: everything grows toward you and nothing is in a hurry. Her temptation is rest — stop walking, be planted, be fed sweet things while the vines decide where you belong.
- **Floors 21–25 — the Lich Lord (Undead).** A cold domain of forbidden knowledge and stolen warmth: bells, libraries, spectral hands. His temptation is curiosity — trade a little warmth, a little feeling, for one more secret, until giving him your pleasure feels like scholarship.
- **Floors 26–30 — the Alpha Beast (Beast).** Wide pack territory of kennels, dens, and musk: floors Syn deliberately designed broad so the pack can roam. His temptation is animal surrender — be chased, caught, and claimed as a mate by a lord too honest to scheme about it.
- **Floors 31–35 — Eros (Goddess of Lust).** Her own stretch, where lust magic is thickest and the reshaping land dreams in her idiom alone. See Floor 35, below.

Passing a boss is passing out of one lord's hands into the next: each domain is a different argument for giving in, made by a different high priest or priestess, in ascending order of power.

## Floor 35 — Throne and Heart

Floors 31–34 are her **dreaming court**: the stretch where the reshaping land moves fastest, half inside her dreams, so rooms arrive half-decided and manifestations are common — altars at every turn, tentacles in the candlelight, a voice that already knows your name. It is less stocked than the domains below and more personal. The danger is not the monsters. It is being individually noticed for four floors.

Floor 35 is the **throne and heart of the temple**: her throne set over the source of the dungeon's lust magic, the well the whole building drinks from. The clergy raised the throne room around that source the way a chapel is raised around a relic. (The ruined altar young Syn knelt at may rest here, carried up and set before the throne — the floor-writing pass can confirm its place, but the symmetry is intended: what Syn found at the bottom of her life, the temple enthroned at the top of her work.)

There is no exit at the top. Win against Eros or surrender to her, floor 35 ends the same way: **she returns you herself**, pleased, and the cycle takes you around again. The last thing a delver learns in a run is that reaching the goddess was never the way out. It is the reason there is no way out.

## What the Bosses Want from Delvers

Every lord wants to **keep** the delver who reaches them. That is the open secret of every boss floor — and temple law, the pilgrimage upward, and finally Eros's cycles take the delver onward anyway. Each domain lives inside that tension: a keeper forever being robbed by the building they helped build. How it plays is different in every idiom:

- **The Broodmother wants to keep you the way she keeps everything:** inside. A delver who reaches her is not a guest or even, in her accounting, a meal — they are brood that has not admitted it yet. Losing them upward is the one theft she has never learned to forgive the temple for.
- **The Succubus Queen wants to be chosen.** Worship, kneeling, desire offered with good manners and eye contact. A kept delver would be ideal, but a delver who leaves still thinking about her is an acceptable tribute — she collects being remembered almost as happily.
- **The Incubus King wants to claim you,** mark the encounter as his, and be begged to repeat it. Possession is the point; duration is a detail his ego fills in afterwards.
- **The Verdant Dryad wants you to stay in the garden** — planted, fed, and slowly becoming part of the grove's long conversation. She loses delvers the way seasons lose leaves and is sadder about it, and for longer, than she ever says.
- **The Lich Lord wants your data and your warmth,** in that order on his paperwork and the reverse order in his hands. A kept delver would be a longitudinal study he could never ethically arrange elsewhere, which is precisely why he arranged to be here.
- **The Alpha Beast wants a mate for the pack's alpha** — caught fairly, kept honestly, added to the warmth of the den. He does not scheme to prevent delvers leaving; he simply assumes the good ones come back, the way weather does.
- **Eros wants surrender.** Not keeping, not service, not even company — the giving-in itself, offered on her own floor, at the top of everything her clergy built. Then she returns the delver personally, because surrender once is a beginning, not an ending.

## Altars & Eros's Attention

The altars were built by the clergy, as offering points raised throughout their temple — and Eros genuinely uses them. Of everything the clergy built on interpretation alone, the altars are where their faith turned out to be simply correct: kneel at one, and she can see you.

**Attention is her literal regard.** Devotion draws her eye: satisfying the tower's creatures, kneeling at her altars, wearing Syn's marks, making offerings. The more a delver lives as an act of worship — whether they know it or not — the more distinctly she notices them among all the delvers in her halls. Attention is not a currency she spends; it is how clearly a particular delver exists to her.

When her regard on a delver grows full, she answers — but only ever in part. A visit is a **partial manifestation**: a white tentacle curling out of the dark, a voice from everywhere, warmth and red eyes in candlelight; a gift of lust-light, her Favour, a curse plucked away, a moment of mercy. She never appears in full below floor 35. What visits a delver is her attention made briefly touchable — proof, for the clergy who built on dreams and signs, that she was listening all along.

This is also the temple's quiet argument with itself: the clergy founded everything on interpretation, yet at the altars the goddess demonstrably responds. Whether that validates everything they built — or only means she enjoys being worshipped — is a question the theology pass should keep open.

## Monsters, Traps & Living Gear

**Monsters are shaped, not born.** The monster-spawning working that Syn and the Incubus King created together draws in raw appetite and shapes it out of lust energy: things with teeth, hunger, and a use for delvers, set loose where delvers walk. That is why the halls never empty and why the stock of a domain always suits its lord — the spell shapes what the land, and its ruler, call for.

Their minds vary. Spawned stock runs mostly on appetite, with flashes of cunning where a domain's idiom rewards it. Above them stand genuinely sapient beings — bosses, and residents who are people rather than stock — who scheme, bargain, keep shops, fall in love, and hold grudges. The line between "monster" and "resident" is sapience, not shape.

**Traps are part of the land.** Syn and the Succubus Queen designed them — with the Verdant Dryad inspiring the tentacle and vine work — but nobody rewinds them by hand. Traps were built into the domains themselves, and the reshaping land re-dreams them along with the rooms: sprung plates reset, vines regrow hungry, and a corridor that caught you yesterday has thought of something new. Syn's philosophy, built into every mechanism: a trap should leave you changed, not just caught.

**Living gear took root.** Syn made the first cursed and living pieces by hand — collars, garments, and toys with appetites of their own. Then her designs took root in the dungeon's lust magic, and the place began producing living gear on its own: in chests, in mimic broods, in dark corners where desire pools. It remembers who wore it, wants to be worn again, and resents removal. Covar Zephyren collects, catalogues, and rehomes the strays from his counter at the Cursed Shop, which makes him less a merchant than a matchmaker between delvers and the gear that has chosen them.

## Theology & the Cycles

Eros's doctrine can be said in one line: **surrender is the offering.** What she ultimately wants from delvers is not service, conversion, or tribute — it is the moment, and the life-long habit, of giving in to lust and depravity. What a surrendered delver becomes afterwards — worshipper, resident, pet, or simply someone who goes around again more honestly — is secondary. Depravity is not the road to her gift; it is the gift's destination.

The **cycles are her answer to the temple.** The clergy built for decades on dreams and signs, never directed and never confirmed. When the work stood finished, Eros responded — her first and only unambiguous act upon the place. She laid the cycles over her temple: delvers who reach the top are not released, only returned, to go around again and again until they give in. Their gift to her was a temple; her gift to them was a trap with no exit but surrender.

The clergy know the cycles are hers, not theirs. They recognise her hand in them, and count them as their one proof that she answered — the founding generation's interpretation, made true exactly once. Everything else they built remains interpretation; the cycles are response.

What Eros thinks of her clergy — and of Syn, who started everything without being asked — is deliberately not decided here. Her approval, amusement, or indifference is the question the temple reveal exists to answer, and every cleric who has ever knelt at her altars has had to live inside that uncertainty.

## How the Clergy View Delvers

Collectively: **with no contempt at all.** Doctrine makes contempt impossible. If surrender is the offering, then the delver who kneels at every altar, the delver whose lust has grown visible fur, and the delver who has been broken on the same floor eleven times are not the temple's failures. They are the temple *working*, at three different speeds.

- **The devoted** — delvers high in her Attention, regular at altars, wearing marks — are recognised the way clergy anywhere recognise their own regulars: by name, by preference, with the particular warmth reserved for proof that the ministry works. Berry feeds them first. Syn inks them with more care than she admits.
- **The transformed** are read like published work. A hybrid form is a delver's desire made legible, and the clergy read them fluently and affectionately: Syn sketches every new shape, the lords of kindred domains take a professional interest, and nobody in the temple pretends a tail is anything but a sentence the body finally finished.
- **The frequently defeated** are regarded almost tenderly. Being broken means giving in, and giving in is the sacrament; a delver who has surrendered forty times has made forty offerings. If individual clerics have private tastes — the Queen finds the too-easy briefly boring, the Lich Lord prizes an unusual defeat the way others prize vintages, the Broodmother considers frequent visitors to her floors practically family — those are appetites, not judgments. Nobody in this temple looks down on a delver for losing. Losing is the local form of prayer.

## The Temple Reveal

Players should not initially be told that the dungeon is a temple.

### Syn and Berry's language rule

Syn and Berry must never call the place a **dungeon**.

Before the temple reveal, they should keep its nature vague and use indirect language such as:

- "the tower"
- "this place"
- "these halls"
- "the floors"
- "up here" or "down there," where appropriate

They should not accidentally confirm that it is a temple before the reveal.

After the truth is revealed, Syn and Berry can openly call it **the temple**.

This restriction applies specifically to Syn and Berry. General narration, system text, other characters, and player-facing labels may continue to use words such as dungeon, ruins, or tower unless a later lore pass decides otherwise.

### Reveal timing

The reveal happens **after the first return**. A delver who reaches floor 35 is sent around by Eros herself — and on the second climb, the pretence ends: she has met the goddess, been handed back, and now knows there is no exit, only the loop. From the second cycle onward, the clergy stop pretending with this delver.

**Syn delivers it**, confirming what the delver has already half-guessed — she is proud of the work, and tired of euphemisms with someone who has seen the throne. The reveal is therefore personal rather than proclaimed: one delver at a time, at the moment they have earned the truth by surviving it. Before that moment, the language rule above holds absolutely. After it, Syn and Berry may call the place the temple to that delver.

## Ask About the Bosses

**Talk to Berry** and **Talk to Syn** buttons on the quest board and at the parlour open a conversation screen for each fairy. Defeating or satisfying a boss unlocks that boss as a topic with both of them. The conversation menu currently holds 17 topics (10 general, 7 bosses); Discord select menus allow 25 options, so there is room to grow.

The same boss should produce different conversations depending on who is asked:

- **Berry** gives the domestic, emotional, and relationship-focused view. She is open about jealousy, affection, discomfort, and devotion.
- **Syn** gives more of the temple's history and explains how the boss contributed to building it, while also revealing her own flirtatious history and professional relationships.

The Alpha Beast is the exception to their disagreement: both Syn and Berry adore him and regard him as a very good boy.

The unlock is based on the bosses the player has defeated or satisfied (the `bosses_seen` record). Either path opens the topic — but **satisfying a boss unlocks extra**: Syn and Berry both have additional things to say about a delver who pleased a lord rather than merely beating them, and satisfying is always the version they ask about first. Conversations can be repeated freely, and the fairies remember previous conversations (see Repeats, Memory & Cycles, below). General topics also unlock from experience — first monster defeated, first trap, first curse, first mark, first hybrid form, first altar offering, first completed cycle.

### After the Reveal

Once a delver has been returned from floor 35 and Syn has confirmed the truth (second cycle onward), boss conversations gain **full revealed variants** — second versions of the topics, not extra lines:

- **Syn speaks as a builder.** The euphemisms end. She can call it the temple, name what each boss contributed, tell founding history, and discuss the clergy as clergy. Her revealed variants are where the temple's real history lives.
- **Berry stays Berry.** She may say "temple" now, and she stops pretending not to know — but she does not become a lore source. Her revealed variants stay domestic: the same wife-and-shopkeeper gossip, finally allowed its proper nouns. Marriage still wins the room over ministry.

### Repeats, Memory & Cycles

The fairies **remember being asked**. A topic's first answer is the full one; asking again gets a shorter, familiar version — a summary with the warmth (or the barb) left in, and occasionally an acknowledgment that this delver has asked before. Nobody delivers the same speech twice at someone they recognise.

Depth also grows with cycles. Beyond the reveal variants, higher cycles unlock warmer and sharper addenda as Syn and Berry stop treating the delver as a customer and start treating them as a regular: more history from Syn, more unguarded domestic truth from Berry, and opinions updated by everything the delver has survived. The conversation system should feel like a relationship with a memory, because in-world it is one.

### Do the Bosses Know?

Yes. Of course they know. The fairies are infamous, delvers repeat things, and several of the lords have heard their own descriptions quoted back at them by flushed delvers who thought they were being clever. Most of the clergy find the gossip mill flattering or funny — in a temple this size, being talked about at the shop is a form of worship too — and nobody with any sense expected Syn, of all people, to keep her opinions private. Berry is the only one who occasionally feels guilty about specific sentences, usually the true ones.

### After a Delver Has Met Eros

Meeting Eros changes how the fairies talk about her — not into casualness, but into **shared experience**. The reverence stays exactly where it was; what changes is that the delver is no longer outside it. Both fairies will ask what she said and what she did, compare it against everything they believe, and speak about the goddess the way people speak about a person they all, now, have in common.
