## The Core Secret

The dungeon is not really a dungeon. It is a temple built to worship **Eros** — the **eldritch Goddess of Depravity**, known to most delvers more politely as the Goddess of Lust.

The six bosses below Eros and **Syn** are her high priests and priestesses. Together, they built the temple as an act of devotion. Its monsters, traps, events, transformations, curses, and repeated descents are intended to tempt visitors, break down their restraint, and drive them toward lust and depravity in Eros's name.

Eros herself is not one of the temple's builders in the same sense. She is the goddess the temple serves and the final presence waiting at its top — and the temple is how she is climbing back into existence.

She runs the shop and quest board, and is a devout worshipper of Eros.

## Origin & Founding

The temple was Syn's idea.

Young Syn found a **ruined outdoor altar of Eros** in the wild ruins — the remains of an older, failed place of worship. **Nobody living knows who failed**; only the stone remembers. They failed because they wanted a **love-goddess**, not depravity — and she starved on the wrong offering. At the moment Syn found it, Eros was on the **brink of being forgotten**: faith thinned to nearly nothing, her power guttering with it. Syn knelt at the altar, and the dreams began. Eros never gave her orders. What Syn received was distant and interpretable: dreams, signs, and desire. Syn chose to read those signs as a calling, and that choice is the founding act of the temple — and the first real food the goddess had tasted in an age. Eros's clergy therefore serves a goddess they interpreted rather than obeyed — a fact that can colour their confidence, their disagreements about her will, and the temple reveal.

The stone was **carried up** into the finished temple and set **before Eros's throne on floor 35** — what Syn found at the bottom of her life, enthroned at the top of her work. When a domain lord is away, that same stone may wake as the **First Altar Stone** relic and hold an empty threshold; its resting place is the throne room. It **claims Syn as its first true worshipper**; she knows, and it embarrasses and flatters her in equal measure. Delvers learn Syn's altar story properly **after the temple reveal**.

Syn then gathered the other six builders herself. Their origins are mixed — some ancient, some elevated, some recruited from elsewhere — but one rule holds for all of them: **each of the six had a personal history with Syn before she recruited them.** Syn did not hire powers or summon strangers; she collected her own past — friends, collaborators, a teacher, former lovers, and at least one person she does not get along with — and persuaded each of them that Eros was worth building for. Why each one agreed is part of their individual history with her.

Per-boss histories (before + why + contribution) and **build chapters** are written under each lord in Syn's Histories with the Clergy.

The building took **decades** — the number is left fuzzy on purpose (fairy time, mortal worry, and nobody kept a clean calendar). The temple was built whole by the clergy: not a repurposed older dungeon, but a structure raised deliberately on the site Syn's discovery had marked, by clergy magic powered by **lust energy** — desire made to do work, the same force the Lich Lord taught Syn to use. Traps, floors, spawning-spells, and altars were not added to a dungeon afterwards; they were built into a temple from the beginning. The place never fully became a “dungeon” in the clergy's mouths: some of them still call rooms by temple names, and the disguise is for delvers, not for the builders.

### Construction — order, firsts, crises

**Recruit / join order** (Syn collecting her past into a priesthood):

1. **Lich Lord** — teacher first; without lust-energy work, there is no temple.
2. **Verdant Dryad** — safe permanence and company.
3. **Succubus Queen** — best friend and trap-wright.
4. **Mimic Broodmother** — endless table.
5. **Incubus King** — debt collected into the spawning working.
6. **Alpha Beast** — because Syn asked; pack-room and mates.

The **first working system** in the unfinished temple was not a trap or an altar — it was **lust-energy load-bearing**: the Lich's lesson made into stone, desire holding weight. Everything else hung on that.

The **biggest near-failure**: the Broodmother nearly ate a work crew — and nearly Syn again. The temple almost ended as a meal. Syn bargained/charmed her way through it a second time in spirit; the mother stayed. The scare is still the story people don't tell delvers.

**Berry** only visited during construction. She moved in when floors began to open — camp follower of one, then wife of a founder with a shop to run.

The first time **all seven founding clergy** were in one room is locked as follows. **Syn** called it — everyone needed to see everyone — at her **temporary camp on the build site**. **Berry was there**, horrified, holding Syn's hand under the table.

The night went as badly as their personalities predicted: the **Incubus King hit on Berry**; the **Lich Lord insulted everyone's intelligence in order**; the **Alpha Beast nearly killed the King on Syn's behalf**; the **Succubus Queen could not stop laughing** the entire time. **Dryad** kept what order there was — fruit and firmness.

What came of it: **floor-assignment arguments began that night**, and **Syn swore she would never host again**. (She has broken that oath for drinks with the Queen. She has not broken it for all seven.)

Eros's part in the building remains deliberately distant. She did not design the temple, direct its construction, or confirm the clergy's interpretation of her signs. What she *did* get from Syn and the finished temple is clearer: **faith restoring her**, worshippers feeding her, power returning with every delver who gives in. What she thinks of Syn in particular is left for the reveal and beyond; that Syn saved her from vanishing is not.
