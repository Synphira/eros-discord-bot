## Syn's Histories with the Clergy

All six are written.

### Mimic Broodmother

Before the founding, the Broodmother was already a wild broodmother: an ancient mimic horror with her brood, and a local legend. Syn was once her kept thing — caught by the brood and kept **inside the chest** as a favourite treat. The Broodmother's tongues teased and pleasured her constantly; she was never eaten, because the mother enjoyed the *taste* of Syn too much to finish the meal. Syn bargained and charmed her way out, which no one does, and the two have respected each other ever since: the mother, because Syn is the one meal that became a person to her; Syn, because being kept taught her exactly what the Broodmother is for. (Berry knows a version of this story as "she once tried to fuck my wife." Berry is not wrong. She is just not complete.)

When Syn returned years later with the temple in her head, the Broodmother did not join out of faith. She joined for the **endless table**: a temple that would deliver delvers to her domain forever, and call her appetite worship. She is a high priestess the way a river is a road — because everyone agreed to use her that way, her included.

**Build chapter:** Soft reunion between meal and person — Syn half-afraid she'd be kept again. Crisis: Syn tested a mimic nursery too close to the site; brood went for the crew. Syn offered **herself as distraction** while they fled (taste, don't finish — again). Aftermath: crew never spoken of, Syn still flinches; Broodmother thought it was funny. Berry was on-site and dragged Syn out personally. Locked image: Syn emerging sticky, laughing too hard.

**Private takes (clergy grid):** Queen = too much talk, not enough keeping. King = loud meat, not worth opening for. Dryad = confused by hospitality without hunger. Lich = cold box, no juice, skip. Alpha = big warm thing, almost brood.

### Succubus Queen

Syn's **best friend** — and the first person in the clergy who recognised her as the same species of disaster. They met long before there was a temple. The Queen's first thought, looking Syn over, was roughly: *how did a succubus turn into a fairy, and why is she as perverted as me?* They hit it off immediately. Fellow artisans of cruelty and pleasure; the flirting, the filthy jokes, the work that mysteriously required both of them in the same room for days — all of that predates the founding and has simply never stopped. Berry endures it the way one endures weather.

Syn still visits the Queen for drinks and hanging out whenever the tower lets her; the Dryad joins them sometimes. In court she is every inch a queen; with Syn she is more genuine — bright, filthy best-friend energy under the velvet. The Queen was already what her name says, at small scale: queen of a small demon court, a real title over a small kingdom. Syn's offer made the kingdom eternal and gave her the commission of her life — co-designing, with Syn, every trap in the temple as the **great work** of her craft. She did not join for Eros. She joined for the masterpiece, and stayed because a court that delivers worshippers forever turned out to suit her. If she has a theology, it is that being worshipped is simply good manners, finally enforced by architecture.

**Madame Vex** once belonged to that court. She fell from it after foolishly sleeping with the Incubus King — everyone knew he does not romance, only take his own satisfaction. The Queen pretends not to know her: *even I don't bother knowing sluts that desperate.*

**Build chapter:** The great work started instantly — traps *were* the friendship. First trap that worked was humiliating and perfect (they high-fived) *and* nearly killed a test delver (they kept it). Her court was mostly ignored; the mechanisms were the real throne. Tension: flirting so constant Berry already hated visits, plus soft jealousy when Syn vanished into Lich labs. The Mirror Queen mirror went up because **Syn dared her**. Locked image: Syn asleep on the Queen's throne mid-design.

King = embarrassing colleague Syn makes her tolerate + useful warning poster for desperate sluts. Dryad = soft third for drinks and mom-friend ballast. Lich = wants to put a mirror in his library for spite. Alpha = Syn's dog, adorable, not court material.

### Incubus King

Before the founding, the King was exactly what Syn calls him: a wandering incubus of genuine talent and no fixed address — gorgeous, gifted, and completely unbearable, in that order and all at once. He also owed Syn a debt from an old disaster, and Syn collects.

**The disaster:** he tried to seduce and sleep with a witch. She cursed him so that anyone who sees his cock laughs uncontrollably. After many failed attempts to live with the curse and break it, he came to Syn crying and begging for her help. She helped — and never, ever forgot. The details are still the story Syn tells when she wants him to leave a room, and the reason he does not argue with her about floors (see How the Floors Were Assigned, below).

She collected the debt in expertise. His half of the monster-spawning working — the appetite-shaping half, the half with flair — was the price of settling what he owed, and it made them professionally inseparable long after the account was closed. Why did he stay and take a domain and a priesthood? For the **endless claim**: five floors that deliver him delvers to possess forever, and a title — high priest, king in a temple — worth posing in. His crowns have always been self-declared. This one just has architecture.

He performs kingship as sleazy charm: naked to show off, always flirting, confident and predatory on his throne. Nobody in the clergy is impressed by the cock display. When Syn is angry, the performance collapses — he goes meek and quiet. Vex was one of many one-night stands; he does not remember her name.

None of the clergy especially like him. Everyone puts up with him: Syn because the debt still has useful leverage, the Queen and Dryad because Syn asks them to, the Lich Lord because complaining wastes data, the Broodmother because he is occasionally edible company, and the Alpha Beast because Syn said so.

**Build chapter:** Debt collected **early** — he was crying at Syn's door before floors existed; she cashed it into the spawn working. Collaboration: Syn competent, him unbearable, results filthy-good; she made him revise until he cried again. He stayed for endless claim **and** fear of Syn telling the laugh-curse story. Floor 15: he tried to charm Berry into lobbying Syn. Berry was already being hit on; Syn was the shield. Locked image: Syn holding a spawn diagram while he poses naked uselessly.

**Private takes (clergy grid):** Broodmother = scary hot (won't admit fear). Queen = Syn's favourite (jealous under the flirting) and would fuck for politics. Dryad = boring fruit lady. Lich = avoids unless Syn forces proximity. Alpha = Syn's attack dog — stays meek; tried to flirt once, never again.

### Verdant Dryad

The quietest friendship in the clergy — and Syn's **mom friend**: the person so naturally caring they feel maternal whether or not you ever call them mother. Syn does not. She just keeps ending up fed, rested, and gently bossed into drinking water. Long before the founding, Syn used to visit one great tree and its grove to think, and to be fed — the Dryad was a rooted, local power who never travelled, and Syn was her most interesting weather. No bargains, no debts, no disasters. When your other relationships are the Broodmother, the Queen, and the King, a friend who gives you fruit and asks nothing becomes load-bearing.

The Dryad did not join for power, and she already had permanence. She joined for **company**. Rooted lives are lonely in a very specific way: everything visits, nothing stays, and centuries pass at the speed of seasons. A temple promised her a garden the world would walk through forever — visitors delivered to her shade in endless variety, conversation, gossip, and the fairies' friendship at walking distance, forever. Her vines' enthusiasm for guests is not hunger, exactly. It is hospitality that has waited a very long time. Alone in her grove she is reserved; with Syn and the Queen she turns cheerful and talkative. She sometimes joins them for drinks; she is the one who remembers to bring fruit. Her appearance follows the seasons (summer is her default; see `art_reference.md`).

**Build chapter:** Years of thinking-naps and fruit, then Syn asked. Offer that landed: a **grove grown inside stone** that would never be lonely. First contributions: vine/tentacle trap ideas sketched in sap, *and* teaching Syn that patience is structure (refusing to hurry the build). Soft tension only — she disliked the King's visits. Berry loved her immediately (fruit bribes). Locked image: first vine-trap test — Syn laughing, sticky.

**Private takes (clergy grid):** Broodmother = worries Syn still smells like her sometimes. Queen = likes drinks nights and watches Syn for dehydration/overstimulation. King = dislikes his visits; protective of Berry around him. Lich = respects Syn's teacher, doesn't visit. Alpha = loves him (grove picnics, shade on purpose, healthiest of Syn's collection).

### Lich Lord

Teacher and student — and, under the insults, something closer to family. The Lich Lord was already ancient when Syn found him: an undead sorcerer a thousand years starved of warmth, hoarding forbidden knowledge in the cold. He taught her how to use lust energy, how to make desire do work instead of merely making noise, and nearly everything the temple's building later required was built on that teaching. Syn was his rare living pupil. She still calls him **old and creepy** to his face, and means both words — and she cares for him the way one cares for a father figure one would rather die than admit having. He will not admit the reverse: that he is proud of her as her teacher, and that he also views her as a daughter. The pride shows in the work he still corrects, in the silences he does not fill with anyone else, and in a tell he cannot quite hide: when Syn impresses him he goes quiet and his frost crown flickers.

On his observation floors he is a researcher obsessed with the data. When he bothers to appear for Syn he usually acts tired and bored, looking down on most of the clergy because he believes he is smarter than everyone else. His voice is an insulting teacher at all times.

He did not join for warmth, whatever his spectral hands suggest. He joined for **the temple as experiment**: thirty-five floors of controlled desire, thousands of delvers, every appetite catalogued under laboratory conditions no solitary sorcerer could ever arrange — and a vantage point from which to watch how the magic twisted over time. He is a high priest the way a naturalist is a gardener. If Eros is real to him at all, she is his largest data set.

**Build chapter:** He found *her* — sniffed out a living pupil. He almost refused to teach; she annoyed him into it. Breakthrough: a collapse he had predicted, which she fixed, and his crown flickered the first time. What he demanded for joining was **Syn's continued study under him** (pride). Berry never went near him in those years. Locked image: the crown's first flicker when she impressed him.

**Private takes (clergy grid):** Broodmother = dislikes how she almost ate the experiment once. Queen = frivolous Syn-time-waste *and* respects trap craft as applied theory. King = lowest IQ, competent spawn-half, Syn's leverage problem — all of it. Dryad = soft respect for patience as structure. Alpha = honest data + Syn's emotional support animal (said dryly).

### Alpha Beast

He was simply the wild alpha of his pack — no titles, no temple — and of everyone Syn has ever collected, he is the only one who has never schemed at her, lied by omission, kept a price in reserve, or wanted anything complicated at all. In a clergy of appetites and agendas, that honesty is why both fairies adore him without qualification.

Why did he take a domain and a priesthood? **Because Syn asked — and what she described was pack-room and mates.** The two reasons are inseparable, and neither required paperwork. Syn designed his floors wide so the pack can roam, the temple delivers him the world in rut and variety, and he repays all of it the way he repays everything: completely, immediately, and by wagging. Soft eyes in a nightmare body; always in rut, gentle with Syn and Berry. He speaks mostly in growls and body language, with a few broken words. ## Floors & Boss Domains

The dungeon is built in seven five-floor stretches. Each stretch is the **domain** of the boss who waits at its end: a personal territory and mini-temple expressing that lord's particular style of temptation. The monsters, traps, and events inside a domain are not random infestation; the land itself serves its lord.

The lords came first. Nobody was recruited to fill a pre-designed domain: Syn gathered the people, and each domain was shaped around its lord afterwards — their appetites, history, and way of tempting, given five floors to become a place. If a domain feels like a portrait, that is because it is one.

### How the Floors Were Assigned

The floor numbers were not chance, and they were not a tidy ladder of power. They were a negotiation Syn refereed — and, in more than one case, settled by favouritism or leverage.

- **Floor 5 — Mimic Broodmother.** She **demanded** it. Lowest domain means the most prey walking through her doors before anyone has learned better. Syn gave it to her.
- **Floor 10 — Succubus Queen.** Syn's favouritism, barely disguised. Best friend gets the court Syn wanted to visit most often without climbing half the tower.
- **Floor 15 — Incubus King.** What was left. He did not argue — he was too scared Syn would tell the disaster he still owes her over.
- **Floor 20 — Verdant Dryad.** She was fine with it. She wanted company, not altitude, and saw no need to argue.
- **Floor 25 — Lich Lord.** He chose it: high enough to **observe the tower and how the magic twisted over time**, cold and quiet enough for the work.
- **Floor 30 — Alpha Beast.** Syn insisted. Her good boy gets the **largest floors** for his pack — wide pack-room as a matter of design, not charity.

The land also **reshapes** strongly between delvers and cycles — layout can flip — but the **idiom stays**. There are **no reliable maps**; only vibe. (Fang charts anyway. The charts lie kindly.) A domain has a personality, not a fixed floorplan.

The stretches grow stronger and filthier the nearer they come to the throne, because their power is **proximity to Eros**: lust magic pools thicker near its goddess. The clergy's graduated design follows that gradient, but the gradient itself is hers.

### Domain flavour (writing bible)

- **Floors 1–5 — Mimic Broodmother.** Cluttered hoard of beautiful junk; brood nurseries of tiny mimics; velvet/wood/gold breath under the mess. Temptation rotates: curiosity, greed, comfort — open it, take it, crawl inside. Cycle colour: glass menagerie ↔ clockwork brothel (and cousins); always things that want to keep you.

- **Floors 6–10 — Succubus Queen.** Fairy-adjacent glamour in Syn's visual language: perfume, glitter, candle gold. Signature spaces are throne and audience chambers. Temptation is **vanity** — see yourself desired. Cycle colour: opera-box decadence ↔ open court orgy with etiquette. When Syn visits for drinks the domain stays filthy; the Queen just laughs more.

- **Floors 11–15 — Incubus King.** Sleazy royal purple and gold, always a little tacky. Harems and display cages that look consensual and aren't. Temptation is **ownership** — the relief of being claimed. Cycle colour: hunting lodge ↔ coronation party that never ends. When Syn is near, the décor goes quieter and meeker; the land flinches with him.

- **Floors 16–20 — Verdant Dryad.** Palette follows her **seasons** (green / red / white / pink blossom). Groves and clearings — including echoes of the wedding grove. Temptation is **slow belonging**: vines decide where you fit. Cycle colour: flooded lily baths ↔ fruit-market maze. When Syn and the Queen drink here the garden stays quiet; the Dryad gets talkative.

- **Floors 21–25 — Lich Lord.** Old paper, frost, candle stubs — library cold. Observation galleries look down the tower. Temptation is **curiosity**: trade warmth for one more secret. Cycle colour: clockwork orrery floors ↔ silent ossuary stacks. When Syn visits, the labs tidy themselves for the pupil.

- **Floors 26–30 — Alpha Beast.** Musk, fur, warm dens, wide open air — floors Syn built broad for the pack. Chase corridors and hunting grounds. Temptation is **rut honesty** — no schemes, just need. Cycle colour: snowfield hunt ↔ summer meadow den. When Syn sits between his ears the pack goes gentle and playful.

- **Floors 31–35 — Eros.** Candle, rose, silk, white tentacles with crimson-magenta undersides. Floors 31–34 are the **dreaming court**: reshaping fastest, half inside her dreams, altars and manifestations common. What scares is personal: being noticed, rooms that know your name, desire that feels like *hers*. Floor 35: throne, First Altar before it, heart of the temple — see below.

Passing a boss is passing out of one lord's hands into the next: each domain is a different argument for giving in, made by a different high priest or priestess, in ascending order of power.

## What the Bosses Want from Delvers

Every lord wants to **keep** the delver who reaches them. That is the open secret of every boss floor — and temple law, the pilgrimage upward, and finally Eros's cycles take the delver onward anyway. Each domain lives inside that tension: a keeper forever being robbed by the building they helped build. How it plays is different in every idiom:

- **The Broodmother wants to keep you the way she keeps everything:** inside. A delver who reaches her is not a guest or even, in her accounting, a meal — they are brood that has not admitted it yet. Losing them upward is the one theft she has never learned to forgive the temple for.
- **The Succubus Queen wants to be chosen.** Worship, kneeling, desire offered with good manners and eye contact. A kept delver would be ideal, but a delver who leaves still thinking about her is an acceptable tribute — she collects being remembered almost as happily.
- **The Incubus King wants to claim you,** mark the encounter as his, and be begged to repeat it. Possession is the point; duration is a detail his ego fills in afterwards.
- **The Verdant Dryad wants you to stay in the garden** — planted, fed, and slowly becoming part of the grove's long conversation. She loses delvers the way seasons lose leaves and is sadder about it, and for longer, than she ever says.
- **The Lich Lord wants your data and your warmth,** in that order on his paperwork and the reverse order in his hands. A kept delver would be a longitudinal study he could never ethically arrange elsewhere, which is precisely why he arranged to be here.
- **The Alpha Beast wants a mate for the pack's alpha** — caught fairly, kept honestly, added to the warmth of the den. He does not scheme to prevent delvers leaving; he simply assumes the good ones come back, the way weather does.
- **Eros wants surrender.** Not keeping, not service, not even company — the giving-in itself, offered on her own floor, at the top of everything her clergy built. Then she returns the delver personally, because surrender once is a beginning, not an ending.

## How the Clergy View Delvers

Collectively: **with no contempt at all.** Doctrine makes contempt impossible. If surrender is the offering, then the delver who kneels at every altar, the delver whose lust has grown visible fur, and the delver who has been broken on the same floor eleven times are not the temple's failures. They are the temple *working*, at three different speeds.

- **The devoted** — delvers high in her Attention, regular at altars, wearing marks — are recognised the way clergy anywhere recognise their own regulars: by name, by preference, with the particular warmth reserved for proof that the ministry works. Berry feeds them first. Syn inks them with more care than she admits.
- **The transformed** are read like published work. A hybrid form is a delver's desire made legible, and the clergy read them fluently and affectionately: Syn sketches every new shape, the lords of kindred domains take a professional interest, and nobody in the temple pretends a tail is anything but a sentence the body finally finished.
- **The frequently defeated** are regarded almost tenderly. Being broken means giving in, and giving in is the sacrament; a delver who has surrendered forty times has made forty offerings. If individual clerics have private tastes — the Queen finds the too-easy briefly boring, the Lich Lord prizes an unusual defeat the way others prize vintages, the Broodmother considers frequent visitors to her floors practically family — those are appetites, not judgments. Nobody in this temple looks down on a delver for losing. Losing is the local form of prayer.
